# Session handoff, 2026-09-08 to 09-10

Written for a session starting cold. `HANDOFF.md` is still the entry point for
the branch as a whole; this file covers what these three days changed, what was
refuted, and what to pick up. 26 commits, branch `data_generation` (merged
`feat/algohex-3d-frame-field` into `master` on the way, **not pushed**).

## What the work is for

Settled explicitly: the block structures are **training data for a
transformer**, filled by TFI in post-processing, then run as a CFD mesh.
There are two routes with different objectives, and conflating them caused
real confusion:

* **standard transformer, first-order** — more blocks are fine, and blocks of
  *comparable size* are preferable. Block-count minimisation is NOT the goal.
* **plan B, higher-order** — the smallest possible block count, with block
  edges carried as cubic curves. Meshtron's `blade-known-reinsertion` branch
  already does the 2D version: `geom_head_prototype.py` regresses four
  chord-local scalars per half-edge as cubic Bezier control points and scores
  them against the true streamline.

Decision log with the alternatives and measurements:
`docs/decisions/2026-09-08-hex3d-block-structure-objective.md`.

## Results that hold

**The v15-v18 input iteration.** The two artificial rings between the cut
surfaces are separable and only one costs the pinch. Deleting both (v14, 310
feature edges) costs 96 inverted cells; deleting only the root ring (v16, 422
edges) removes the pinch just as completely for 32. The rings are not
artefacts: the hub ring's median dihedral kink is 81°, 86 of its 112 edges
clear AlgoHex's own 70° threshold. `tet_prep_v5` gained
`--merge-interfaces {all,hub}` and `--ring-kink DEG`.

**A collapse-guard bug that had been costing six blocks.** The sheet-collapse
guard rejects a sheet that makes the mesh worse *than it is now*, so repairing
the mesh first RAISES the bar. The pre-collapse untangle, added while chasing
v14, lifts v11 from 21 inverted / −0.4938 to 6 / −0.3116, and the collapse that
reached 16 blocks before it existed stopped at 22 with it. The bar is now fixed
at the raw AlgoHex quality for the whole collapse, and v11 is back to 16 blocks
cell-for-cell.

**TFI is a generator.** Prescribe h, get a mesh: `solve_block_divisions` (MILP
over direction classes) plus `refill_block`. Identity refill returns every
boundary vertex to 2.2e-15. Resampling is parameterised by normalised INDEX,
not arc length, which is what makes the complex weld.

**The pipeline generalises to unseen geometries.** Six of the 21
`data/random_tistos/` runners ran end to end. One change was needed
(`tet_prep_v5 --msh`); the candidates declare two physical groups fewer than
T1_9, which is harmless because `classify` reads geometric entity ids and those
are identical. The generated geometries are EASIER than T1_9: 4 raw inverted
cells against 21, 31 raw blocks against 117, no pinch, 22 blocks with
100 % cuboids in five of six samples. T1_9 is an awkward member of its family,
not a representative one.

**Direction classes are the flow directions.** Decomposed into the cylindrical
basis, every sample has exactly ONE radial class spanning every block, 28-29
cells, axis lengths within 1.02x; 3-4 circumferential classes at component
0.96-0.98; the meridional direction split over 4-5 plus 2-3 mixed, which is the
passage curving. This makes `h_map` meaningful (h per physical direction) and
gives a learned model a stable label rather than an index.

**`-n` costs AlgoHex almost nothing and dominates our stage.** Per-stage
timings: integrability and parametrization are 8 of the 10 minutes and are
independent of n, because the parametrization computes a MAP and quantization
scales it to the cell count afterwards; extraction costs seconds. n=2000
reproduces v11's structure exactly (16 blocks, 14 cuboids, 11 classes, same two
pinches) from 30x fewer cells, and `clean_blocks` drops from ~45 min to ~1.
Per sample 43 min → about 17. Not a free knob: n=8000 gives a DIFFERENT
structure (19 blocks, 9 classes, no pinch), so several valid collapse endpoints
exist and the resolution picks one.

**Curved block edges are worth 5-8x — in the generative case only.** With a
fine mesh to resample, cubic edges buy 1.00-1.01x, because `_lerp_axis` already
runs through every original vertex. Where a model emits only block corners, the
comparison is against a straight chord:

| divisions kept | chord p95 | coons p95 | gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 % | 0.08172 | 0.01059 | 7.7x |
| 50 % | 0.08009 | 0.01103 | 7.3x |
| 25 % | 0.08056 | 0.01533 | 5.2x |
| 12.5 % | 0.07895 | 0.04065 | 1.9x |

A CFD mesh sits at the TOP of that table (v11's core is ~25 cells per block
edge), where the gain is largest. `curved_refill.py`.

**reattach had two real bugs.** The parts were never welded, and the
wall-normal tanh distribution was computed per BLOCK, so neighbouring layer
blocks agreed at the interface and at the wall and disagreed on all 15 layers
between: 44 boundary-layer blocks with zero shared vertices. Both fixed
(per-vertex clustering, coordinate weld); the layer now has 68 internal
adjacencies and the assembled mesh merges 71 → 19 blocks. Also
`interface_gap` compared centroids and overstated the O-grid gap by an order of
magnitude (0.0218 against a true 0.00214).

**Structured beats the source mesh**, one implementation over both
(`mesh_quality.mixed_metrics`): 56 non-orthogonality violations against 5105,
skewness p95 0.048 against 0.472, 0 face-weight violations against 34 — with
37 % fewer cells. Aspect ratio is worse by design (17 boundary-layer layers).

## Refuted, including three of my own claims

1. **"v14's collapse stalls because residual inverted cells disarm the
   guard."** Iterating the untangle takes v14 to 13 inverted and min sJ −0.30
   and it still stops at exactly 217 blocks. Structural, not quality-gated.
2. **"Block edges sit where the geometry bends, so dissolving one makes the
   refill interpolate across the bend."** Every merged group refills on its own
   with the same boundary error as its members or better (0.6-1.0x).
3. **"Merging destroys boundary fidelity."** See the correction below — it is a
   weld bug.
4. **The feature-curve merge constraint** rejects the merges that WORK
   ([4,5,7,13] and [9,11]) and not the one that was blamed ([4,9,13,15], which
   crosses no feature edge at all). Off by default, kept with the measurement.
5. **Checkpoint reuse** (`--hexex-in-path`), documented as skipping 86 % of the
   runtime, was never tested and fails immediately: AlgoHex writes the
   intermediate tet mesh as binary OVM and its own `-i` reader rejects it.

## The open thread, and where to start

**The merged structures fail on a WELD, not on arithmetic.** Counted on the
refilled meshes: v11m has 12 482 boundary faces against v11's 9 864, with FEWER
cells. A coarser mesh has less boundary, not more, so 2 618 of those are
interior faces whose sides did not weld; their vertices sit inside the domain
and produce the boundary error of 0.174 that was previously blamed on direction
classes. Measured directly, v11m's boundary faces at the solved counts are
BETTER than v11's (p95 0.00493 against 0.00625).

`tfi.check_watertight` misses it: it tests for faces used by three or more
cells, and a seam that comes apart produces extra faces used by ONE. **Add a
boundary-count balance to it, find where the 2 618 faces are, and re-run the
12-block refill.** Until that is done, "can 12 blocks give a good CFD mesh" is
open — and the evidence now points to yes.

## Other open items

* **Block extraction at very low n.** n=100 (127 hexes) and n=500 have valid
  AlgoHex output; `clean_blocks` on them produced neither a file nor a visible
  error, which was being investigated when the session ended. This bounds how
  low `-n` can go, which matters because n=50 was proposed.
* **Block-size uniformity** is a criterion nobody has measured, and the
  first-order route wants it. v11 spans 224 to 11 900 cells, a factor of 53.
  The lever is `--collapse-rounds`: the raw base complex has 117 blocks, three
  rounds take it to 16, and somewhere between lies more blocks of more even
  size. Cheap now that n can be small.
* **The runner solid.** `tet_prep_solid.py` works and is the complement of
  `tet_prep_v5` — nothing is cut out, nothing re-attached, 170 lines. The first
  AlgoHex run diverged after 90 minutes (623 000 KKT errors) because the label
  boundaries miss 226 of the 526 sharp edges: RUHUB/RU_HUB_FIX/RUBLADE are
  material groups, and the blade's trailing edge sits in the MIDDLE of RUBLADE.
  With `--sharp 40` adding those creases it got to 14 KKT errors and ran for 12
  hours into extraction, but the IGM shows 1.5 million entries at exactly 2^64
  — an integer overflow, not bad conditioning. Retry coarse (`-n` small) and
  with more constraints before anything else.
* **canada.** `scripts/dtoo_mesh_only.py` builds just the mesh of a dtOO case
  (no decompose, no simpleFoam); needs `LD_LIBRARY_PATH=/dtOO-install/lib`.
  Verified on `test/canadaLight` (24 MB, 177 777 nodes). `demo/canada` fails
  earlier in `bVOAnalyticGeometryToFace::preUpdate` — `aS_skinCorpus_farMid`
  yields 3 faces where the case declares 14. canadaLight has 94 hex regions and
  4 tet regions against T1_9's one tet region plus O-grid, so "which regions are
  the O-grid" stops being a constant. Its bounded volumes include `ru_gridGmsh`
  (the direct analogue of our passage) and `ru_mechGridGmsh` (the solid).
* **The label resolver** (task 12): replace the hard-coded geom ids with
  prefixed physical names, a per-case JSON, then the T1_9 constants as
  fallback. The solid run showed it must also answer "where do the feature
  edges come from here", not only "which id means what": label boundaries are
  the feature curves on the PASSAGE, not in general.
* **v18's post-processing** never ran; its raw AlgoHex output is on disk and
  interesting (`--full-constraints` halves the raw block count on a 310-edge
  input, 242 → 120).

## Machine

2-vCPU Hostinger VPS with a fair-use CPU limit on SUSTAINED load. Twelve hours
of two parallel `clean_blocks` runs got it throttled to ~10 % of its own cores
(steal 90 %); the user had to reset the limit. Use `/root/bin/capped` (outside
the repo, machine-specific): `capped -- <cmd>` for one full core,
`capped --duty 45/15 -- <cmd>` to add a 15-minute pause every 45. Docker is not
covered — use `run_algohex.py --cpus N`. Check `vmstat 1 3` column **17** for
steal before starting anything long. **Dataset generation is going to the
cluster; do not start long batches here.**
